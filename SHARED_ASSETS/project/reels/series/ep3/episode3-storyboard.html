Night Terror: Dead Roads · Episode 3 · storyboard v1 · build: Codex

Tunnel Vision

Episode 2 smashed to black as the Stalker leapt at the rider. Episode 3 reveals what really hit in that black: Sam's Night Terror, at full speed, from the side. The two survivors meet, the Stalker gets up, and a chase through the street ends in a tunnel. The Stalker loses an antler at the mouth. The way ahead is full of parked cars, and someone is sitting in every one.

Why this episode works

It pays off the Ep2 cliffhanger with a twist: the hit in the black was Sam's car, not the Stalker. That turns a cheap cut into a reveal, and it finally puts Ep1's hero and Ep2's rider in the same frame, so the series has a crew. The Stalker takes its first real wound and stays alive, which makes it a recurring villain with a grudge. The tunnel full of seated, silent infected escalates from one monster to a whole hidden population, and it costs little to render because a single headlight lights it.

Sound and music

Score map

  • Score: make it in Flow Music, continuing the locked v5 sound (orchestra, original 80s synth, real recorded electric guitar doubled by bass). No AI-guitar distortion, and no cutting the music out suddenly. Keep 120 BPM through Acts I–III so the cut feels continuous with Ep2.
  • 0–3.5 s: no music. Breath, rain, then the V8 rising from left to right before we see it.
  • 3.5 s: the impact is the downbeat. Full score enters on the T-bone.
  • 8–20 s: score drops to a low pulse and synth bed. Truck groan and click-growl sit on top.
  • 20–33.5 s: full drive. The Screamer scream starts the bar, the roof claw hits land on beats.
  • 33.5–36 s: the tunnel swallows the sound. Low-pass sweep, the score rings out naturally over 2 bars (no hard stop).
  • 36–46.5 s: no music. Engine ticks, wind through claw holes, a dripping pipe, tyres creeping on grit.
  • 46.5–48 s: one sub hit on the cut to black, then the Stalker's click from far behind.
  • SFX sources: Freesound / Openverse CC0 checked with earshot (see xc\dead-roads-ep2\audio). Stalker voice is the designed Ep2 voice, never a stock monster growl.
Style and realism rules

Look

  • Found footage: every ground shot is handheld documentary at eye height, with operator micro-shake, late reframes, slight focus breathing and motion blur. Never drone-smooth. Three shots (4, 13, 16) are the dash camcorder itself: VHS softness, chroma bleed, head-switch noise on the bottom 8 px, a small REC ● 3:47 AM in the corner and nothing else.
  • Signature moves Sam loves: the low side-follow keeping the body centred (shots 3, 14), the crash zoom over a roof to the creature's face and fast pull-back (shot 9), and the 20 mm camera on the asphalt at the tyre (shots 8, 15).
  • No slow-mo in this episode. Ep2 already used it. Everything is real speed. The J-turn is one smooth motion with no stop-start.
  • Lights: the car's left headlight is dead from shot 5 on and stays dead. No flickering or toggling lights anywhere.
  • Humans: perfect anatomy. The pull-up in shot 6 is a forearm grip, so no fingers splay. Relaxed hands hang palm to thigh. Compare against real reference photos before sending a frame.
  • Realism: wet scanned asphalt, ordinary messy streets, sensor noise in the dark, lens smudge in rain, AgX grade without over-saturating. Nothing glossy or symmetric.
  • Render: Blender 5.0.1 portable xc\blender501\blender.exe, OptiX only, adaptive sampling, OptiX denoiser, reduced bounces, persistent data. One GPU render at a time across both AIs.
