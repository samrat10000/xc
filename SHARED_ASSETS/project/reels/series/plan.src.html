Night TerrorDead Roads

At 3:12 AM the city falls. Sam's three friends are stuck in three other cities, and their phones go quiet one by one. Sam builds the Night Terror in one night and drives out to bring them home, one rescue per season.

Every episode is 15 to 20 seconds long with one big moment and ends on a cliffhanger. It uses the same look as the V6 reel: sketch ink that turns into pixel art, the same HUD, and sound synced to the action. Everything is built once from reusable GLB assets, so later episodes are mostly new cameras and new story.

How the scenes will look

These are mockups drawn on the reel's 180×320 HUD grid, using your real Night Terror (taken from the hero render and quantised to the palette). The final episodes come out of the Blender pipeline with proper zombie models, so the lighting and motion will be much better than this. The layout, HUD and hook placement are what to judge here. There is no dithering anywhere, only flat palette colours.

Production plan

Build the library once, then shoot episodes from it

Most of the work goes into the asset library up front. After that, an episode is a script, a few cameras and a render. These are the tricks that keep rendering cheap:

  • Instanced hordes. Blender Geometry Nodes scatters one zombie mesh hundreds of times. Each copy gets a random colourway, walk-cycle offset, scale and facing direction. 250 zombies cost about as much memory as 5.
  • Render at pixel resolution. Pixel frames render at 270×480 (the LESSONS rule), which is about 1/16 of full HD. Only sketch frames need full resolution.
  • Pixel art hides cheap physics. Smash hits are simple rigid-body tosses with a spin. At 4 px per pixel nobody can tell them apart from ragdolls.
  • Reuse cameras and lighting. Each set ships with 6 to 8 saved cameras and a light rig, previewed with PREVIEW=cams.json before any full render.
  • One clock drives everything. The same timeline.py approach syncs the kill counter, the hits, the shake and the sound, so the sound design doesn't drift.
  • Spend the budget on tentpoles. EP 1, 5, 7, 10 and 12 get the longest renders. The quiet episodes reuse sets and have smaller casts.

Launch + cadence

Trailer first, then one episode a day

  • Day 0: the trailer. It tests whether people care before we commit all 12 episodes. The trailer is built from the EP 1, 5 and 7 tentpole shots, so nothing is wasted.
  • Bank 5 episodes before EP 1 goes out. A daily series that skips a day loses its momentum.
  • Same time every day on both IG and YouTube, with an "EP n" badge on every thumbnail so the order is obvious in the grid.
  • Side reels between episodes. "How it works" explainers on the car's parts (ram bar, LED matrix, tyres) reuse the V6 reel format and send people back to the series.
  • Hook rules. The hook text is on frame 0. The last 2 seconds tease the next episode. The end card reads "EP 4 TOMORROW".
  • Comment prompts that matter: "Bite or scratch?", "Which friend next?". Later, viewers vote on the route.
  • Pin and group. Pin the trailer + EP 1 on IG and keep a YouTube Shorts playlist in order, so new followers can binge it.
  • Later: when auto-posting starts, the day becomes 1 episode + 2 side reels + 1 build clip, each previewed with you first.
