Shot wall

Director's rules

  • Centre-punch framing. Every kill is framed so the head sits on the frame's centre line. The audience's eye never has to search; the bullet goes where they're already looking.
  • Shots land on beats. All trigger pulls sit on 120 BPM beats. The four drift kills are four straight beats. The picture is cut to the music clock, not the other way round.
  • Freeze-time is one continuous shot. Shot 4 never cuts: time slows to about 4% (rain hangs, the round barely creeps), the camera keeps drifting, then time snaps back from the same camera.
  • Real slow-mo only. Shots 4 and 10 are rendered with Blender time remap at 120 fps equivalent, never frame-blended.
  • No slideshow moves. No sweeps, slides or orbit cuts. Every cut is motivated: on a muzzle flash, on a match of movement, or on the beat.
  • Nothing stands still. Walkers sway, breathe and shuffle. The Stalker tilts its head, its eyes ignite, its jaw unhinges. Wheels roll the correct way with springy suspension.
  • No title text over the best frames. Only the hook line on frame 0 (it's the thumbnail), small and inside the safe zone. No end card.
  • Sound never drops out by accident. The only silence is the planned one in shot 7, and it fades in. The final black has one sound under it.
