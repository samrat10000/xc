### 1. Bosai musen (the 5 PM chime): https://www.amusingplanet.com/2020/06/bosai-musen-japans-5-pm-chime.html
- Called bosai musen ("disaster wireless") or goji no chaimu ("the 5 PM bell"). Local governments pick the melody; the daily test runs ~5 PM in most places (some at noon, some noon + ~6 PM).
- Songs: "Yuyake Koyake", "Kaero kaero to", "Akatonbo", also "Moon River", "Colonel Bogey March", "Edelweiss".
- Installed ~50 years ago after the **1964 Niigata earthquake**. 9 in 10 towns have one. J-Alert (2007) feeds into it.
- Kids use it as the signal to go home before dark.
- One Ishikawa town broadcasts "Good morning!" at 7 AM and reminders about garbage and being kind to children (great creepy detail: the speaker giving instructions).
=> USED: 1964 is now the year the door first opened; the system was built to keep it shut.

### 2. El Cronista "alarma de la muerte": https://www.cronista.com/usa/trending/todos-los-dias-suena-la-misma-cancion-en-japon-a-las-5-de-la-tarde-y-es-conocida-como-la-alarma-de-la-muerte
- Tune "Yuuyake Koyake" by Shin Kusakawa (1893-1948). Plays ~30 seconds.
- Page does NOT explain the "alarm of death" nickname (unsourced) but shows the chime already has a spooky reputation online.
- Warning sounds: missile = 14-second siren; earthquake = bell; tsunami = fire-truck siren. J-Alert in 5 languages.
=> USED: 30 s chime + answer 13 s later. Idea: different siren types could mean different things coming through.

### 3. Exit 8 (anomaly game -> hit film): https://automaton-media.com/en/news/the-exit-8-movie-producer-on-how-a-game-without-a-story-became-japans-highest-grossing-live-action-film-of-2025/
- A $4 indie game with almost no story (spot anomalies in a looping Tokyo subway passage). Film became one of Japan's biggest of 2025 (the article's figures are inconsistent, so don't quote them).
- The film added a personal life decision for the hero that mirrors the loop (push on or turn back).
=> USED: one hidden anomaly per reel; Sam's personal choice (his mother).

### 4. Analog horror on TikTok: https://theconversation.com/analog-uncanny-how-this-weird-and-experimental-side-of-tiktok-is-forging-the-future-of-horror-222882
- #analoghorror 2.3B views, #liminalspaces 4.9B views (at time of writing, ~2024).
- Traits: degraded VHS look, uncanny everyday places, human-like creatures. "Participatory experimentation": Bitesized Nightmares turned viewers' comments into new videos.
=> USED: VHS town-broadcast cards; viewers suggest rules.

### 5. Rule horror: https://handwiki.org/wiki/Rule_Horror
- Started with "Zoo Rules Horror" (Chinese forum A Island, late Nov 2021, user phKeaLx). No plot, terse rules, rule sets CONTRADICT each other (staff say a building "does not exist"). Spread to web novels, short videos, games. Read as an allegory of bureaucracy.
=> USED: two contradicting rule lists (notebook vs official town notice).

### 6. Kisaragi Station: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisaragi_Station
- 2004, 2channel: a woman ("Hasumi") posts LIVE from a train that won't stop; it halts at a station that doesn't exist; parents can't find it on a map; a one-legged man shouts "Don't walk on the track!"; her last post: battery nearly dead; she vanishes.
- Why it spread: live and ongoing, forum users talked to her in real time, fan theories, follow-up posts. Later: 2020 viral song, 2022 film, replica tickets sold out in an hour.
=> USED: every reel is Sam's live "Day N" post with battery %; viewers reply.

## Instagram research for 5 O'CLOCK (from the Chrome thread "Identify 2D reel art style", 2026-11-22 ~16:47 IST)
