# Motion2D production QA learnings

Field notes from making Episodes 1–2 of 5 O’CLOCK. Each item describes a reusable trigger, a check, and a corrective action. Episode examples are evidence for the rules and can be swapped for examples from another production. This is a candidate companion to the skill's QA checklist.

## Checks to run before a full render

| Error category | Trigger and symptom | Prevention and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contact and mesh penetration | A body is supported by bedding, a hand rests on a prop, or fabrics overlap a moving joint. A fingertip appears detached even when the wrist reaches its exact target. | Review native-size crops of the support side and the free side at first contact, maximum load, an in-between, and the settle. Inspect every digit's visible silhouette and its shadow. Check the visible mesh and the contact surface. IK measurements are useful evidence for reach but cannot detect a mesh intersection. Move or deform the support surface, adjust finger pitch and recheck. |
| Anatomy and weight | A rig is posed from target locations. The chain reaches, but the thumb turns into the cushion, the unweighted hand hovers, or the pose feels stiff. | Inspect wrist roll using the knuckle plane. Compare shoulder → elbow → wrist and the actor's center of mass with real movement footage. Give the free hand a separate arc and a readable settle. Measure support drift across the entire loaded interval. |
| Small narrative clues | The beat depends on mirrored text, a battery percentage, handwriting, or a fragment seen through glass. Correct geometry may still produce an ambiguous clue. | Check at the intended display size, at native crop size, and at the first frame the audience needs the clue. Compare reflected lettering to the original glyphs, including digit order. Test whether the clue reads before the cut. Support it with one visual cue that survives at phone size. |
| Glass and reflected inserts | Dark glass shows reflected character art that looks like a powered screen, slips across the surface, or leaks past its rounded edge. | Use a reflection camera or another geometrically consistent projection. Check surface silhouette plus occluders, peek angles, glass tint and local reflections. Inspect both views side by side. Test the black point and edge falloff separately from the room grade. Keep a weak, environment-specific glass cue where it helps the read. |
| Lighting and values across cuts | A bright room cuts to a dark insert. An average over both can pass while either pose falls outside a reasonable range. | Measure main view and insert individually, then the complete clip. Compare dominant light direction and grade against the locked shot. Record scene-specific targets before judging the clip, and give bright practicals enough highlight range without washing out the rest. |
| Projection, margins and timing | A close prop fits at one key frame but clips during camera motion or falls behind mobile controls. | Check the motion extremes between keys. Measure screen-space bounds for the full frame sequence and inspect native-size first/last frames. Overscan painted backgrounds for drift. Confirm the actual aspect ratio, start/end frame numbers and duration after encoding. |
| Layer hierarchy and reveal | An atmospheric entry has multiple mountains, drifting clouds, and a large creature. The camera can flatten the scale or the clouds can hide the story beat. | Verify each depth plane separately and log its relative image-space travel. Pin creature and ridge to distinct depth bands. Check the initial occlusion, first recognition frame and final hold at delivery size. Leave an environmental scale cue next to the reveal. |
| Motion and continuity | A correct pose appears once while in-betweens pop or loose fabric freezes. | Sample every hold boundary and motion extreme. Compare the same costume, prop and light direction on both sides of the cut. Test that locked content stays unchanged when revising a narrowly scoped effect. |
| Render paths and reproducibility | Blender resolves a relative output path against a different drive root, or a retry silently overwrites a version. | Resolve absolute render paths before rendering. Validate the encoder input count, use a new delivery version and keep the exact source and environment entry point. Reuse locked layers for targeted fixes and verify the reuse by hash. |
| Imported rig state | A reused hero resets to a T-pose or a child accessory drifts after the parent is reposed. Clearing the armature action leaves animation on independent object controls. | Audit animation data on the armature, shape keys, control empties and accessories. After any reset, inspect the first key, an in-between and the last key. Rebuild joint directions and inspect ear pieces, straps and other attached meshes against the body. |
| Canonical asset continuity | A substitute silhouette looks plausible in isolation but the returning character no longer reads as the same design. | Find the latest actual approved mesh, render pass or plate before rebuilding. Compare against the canonical still. If distance or lighting changes, regrade and place the existing asset where practical. |
| Fine edges and artwork resolution | A bright crack or edge looks clean in a small contact sheet but develops staircase pixels against the sky at delivery size. | Open a native-size crop with the final composite and contrast. Draw thin painted graphics oversampled and downsample once with an anti-aliasing filter. Recheck both the line and the luminous halo. |

## Evidence from the current production

- **Alarm, probe 1:** The handset clipped and the battery was too small. Reframing and a larger status mark made both readable. The master also verified minimum side clearance across all 72 frames.
- **Wrong room, probes 1–4:** The duvet intersected knees, an elbow chain missed the support target, and a loose hand penetrated the thigh or hovered. Removing the overlapping cover, solving wrist roll, and treating the support and free hands separately improved the pose.
- **Wrong room, render review:** A 40% preview concealed a pinky intersecting the cushion. A native crop exposed it even with submillimetre IK error. An 8 mm wrist lift and a shallower contact angle corrected the remaining visible split. This is the clearest reason to run native contact crops before the full sequence.
- **Wrong room, revision 2:** The optical reflection stayed in sync but could read as imagery playing on a display. A faint warm window reflection and a small lift to the dark fringe make the glass clearer. Keep the original room sequence locked while comparing this targeted change.

- **The crack, blocking:** Clearing all bone transforms restored a T-pose on the imported hero. A parent empty also retained an animation track that displaced an ear cup after the head started moving. Rebuild the intended joint pose explicitly, inspect animation data on descendants and controls, and check at least two separated frames after clearing inherited animation.
- **The crack, continuity:** The first painted crown lost the original character's shape. Search for an up-to-date canonical asset before making a substitute. Reusing the exact crown cel preserved the horn mouth sizes, asymmetry and radial framework.
- **The crack, window reveal:** A correct outdoor asset can still disappear behind a lintel or curtain. Test the reveal through the actual foreground matte. Include first recognition and late reveal frames in the board and a native crop before the main render. Moving the character to the lower foreground, then separating the haze, retained depth and let the horns read.

## Token and time savings applied to this reel

- Carry approved versions, cut times, and shared asset paths in a compact state file. Reopen the exact shot card and the relevant QA category instead of dumping old logs.
- Run one scripted build, paint, composite, verification and encode path. Keep machine-readable metadata and tail the useful errors. Avoid asking the agent to parse a render line for each frame.
- Reuse canonical rigs, render passes, existing set materials and test scripts. Cache painted and rendered layers. For a compositing fix, revise the affected frames and verify unchanged regions by hashing.
- Render six small keys and one native crop at high-risk moments before queuing the full job. Review a contact sheet first and open only the full-size crops that affect the pass decision.
- Reuse the same reusable set or shot tool within a scene, then move the camera, pose, lighting and story-specific effects for each beat. Keep reference photographs and their observations together.

## Findings applied during the remaining shots

| Error category | Symptom in this reel | Immediate correction and a reusable check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| World and surface orientation | A mirrored phone clock and koban sign looked like export mistakes when reviewed out of context. Nested views belong to different sides of the story. | Label the world and viewing surface in the shot card. Make a source/reference crop for an asymmetric sign or numeral, and confirm the intended number of reflections after projection. Keep the normal-world feed and reversed environmental writing independent. |
| Geometry in close inserts | A bent page showed front lettering on the reverse. The tightly isolated forearm could expose a cut end. | Treat page sides as separate surfaces and verify the sequence immediately before, at and after the turnover. Keep enough connected clothing and body geometry to cover the entire motion envelope before hiding the rest. |
| Layered visibility and contact | An open door hid the notebook after the interior cut. The shoulder touch and the woman's face competed in the window. | Review camera transitions with foreground objects enabled. Hide the obstructing door only after cutting inside. Move Mirror-Mom's pose and lock her fingertip target to the reflected shoulder. Check the contact silhouette at native size. |
| Cost per scene | The street spent minutes constructing separate window trim, road details and houses before drawing any action. | Group static meshes by material and bevel width at scene build. Keep actor rigs, moving horns, and shadows independent. Time one low-resolution frame first, and keep the producer's logs to counts and errors. |
| Reveal inside a small inset | The mirror clue in the phone feed was lost when its inner image sat too far back and cropped the figures. | Verify all nested image boundaries and use the actual phone insert as the review surface. Tune the source camera and move the clue into the mirror area, then recheck it at target size. |
| Cloth and costume under extreme poses | Skin flashed through the hoodie elbow when Sam covered his ears. | Evaluate the pose extreme, not just the silhouette at rest. Remove only concealed arm skin faces where clothes are opaque and preserve skin on the wrist and fingers. |
| Cut order and scene grade | The shot manifest listed 10 before 04, and an early-room grade falsely flagged dark phone inserts and dusk. | Sort by ordinal and assert the running frame offset against each authored start time. Preserve one grade within each shared set and compare intentional lighting changes by cut. Run black-frame checks against a timecoded list of intentional blacks. |

The finishing script also runs three independent frame workers. A 20-frame test at full resolution took 7.02 seconds with the workers and 15.75 seconds serially on the same machine. The PNG hashes matched for all 20 frames. Keep the serial implementation available while benchmarking a new compositor change, and compare outputs before adopting the faster path.

The six-frame street probe dropped from 3:13 to 0:20 on this workstation after static mesh batching and the revised block. These were production runs with different blocking, so use the timings as a guide when diagnosing startup cost. The complete-render runner waits for the GPU to be available so another production can keep its place.
